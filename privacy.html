Grumbit

Privacy policy

Effective 25 November 2026

The short version. Grumbit has no accounts and never asks for your name, email or contacts. The game asks your year of birth once, keeps only an age group on your device, and uses it to protect younger players. Your monsters stay on your device and in your own iCloud. The game sends gameplay statistics and crash reports, tied to a random ID for this installation rather than to your name or an account (the App Store's privacy label still counts them as linked to you, because of that ID), which you can switch off in Settings. If you are signed in to Game Center, your achievements go to Apple's Game Center and, if you are 13 or over, so does your Weekly Rumble score, which appears on a leaderboard under your Game Center name. Ads come from Google's ad network, AdMob, and other networks that bid through it, which collect device information to show and measure them. Ads are only personalised if you are 13 or over and allow tracking when iOS asks. Players under 13 never get personalised ads and are never asked to be tracked.

This policy covers the iOS and iPadOS game Grumbit ("the game"), made by Vlad Berila, an independent developer ("I", "me"). Questions go to vladutz_b_05@yahoo.com.

1. The age question

Grumbit is a game for a general audience that includes children. It is not in the App Store's Kids Category and is not aimed only at children. Because ads and analytics are treated differently for younger players, the very first screen asks "What year were you born?" before anything else happens.

The year is used once, on your device, to work out an age group: under 13, 13 to 17 or adult. Only the age group is kept, on your device. The year itself is never stored, never leaves your device and is not in your iCloud save. The age group is not an account detail; it is not linked to your name or contacts. One related fact, for completeness: the age group itself is not sent as a gameplay statistic (the statistics record only that the question was answered, and how). For a player under 13, though, what the game sends to Google says so, because that is how the protections in section 5 work: every ad request is marked as child-directed (section 4), and Firebase is told to turn ad personalisation off for that installation, which it keeps with the random installation ID described in section 3. Nothing the game sends tells a teen from an adult.

Teens and adults can correct their age group later in Settings. The under-13 group cannot be raised in the game on that device, so a child cannot skip the protections by changing it; if it was chosen by mistake, delete the game and install it again.

2. What stays on your device

Your monsters, coins, gems, collection, settings and progress are saved on your device, inside the game's own storage. The Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets read and update the same save on your device through an App Group; they have no network access of their own. When iCloud is on, a copy of the save is kept in your own iCloud account (iCloud key-value storage) so your game follows you to your other devices; Apple stores it and I never see it. Deleting the game deletes the copy on the device.

The names you give your monsters stay on your device and in your own iCloud. They are not sent to me, to any advertising or analytics partner, or to Game Center.

3. Gameplay statistics and crash reports

To learn where players get stuck and how to improve the game, Grumbit sends gameplay events to Google Analytics for Firebase. These are things like "the first egg was chosen", "a drill ended with this score", "a scuffle was won", "a Weekly Rumble fight was lost", "a prowl came home", "a form evolved", "a second monster moved in", "a video was offered or watched", "a session lasted this long" and "a purchase was made" (with the product; Firebase's own automatic purchase event can add its price and currency). For a player under 13 it also carries the setting that turns ad personalisation off (section 1).

Along with those events, Firebase records a random identifier for this installation of the game, your device model and iOS version, your approximate region (derived from your IP address) and the game's language, and it may record Apple's identifier for vendors (IDFV). It does not receive your name, email, contacts, precise location, your monsters' names or Apple's advertising identifier.

You can switch this off at any time in Settings → Privacy → Share gameplay statistics. From that moment nothing more is sent: not the game's events, not Firebase's automatic ones and not crash reports. Google processes this data as described in Firebase's privacy information. Individual event records are deleted after at most 14 months.

Crash reports

If the game crashes, Firebase Crashlytics sends a crash report: which part of the game's code was running, the device model, iOS version, free memory and storage, and the same kind of random installation identifier. It is used only to find and fix the crash, and it is switched off together with the gameplay statistics.

4. Advertising

Grumbit shows two kinds of ads, supplied by Google AdMob, Google's mobile advertising network. For each ad, AdMob can ask other ad networks to bid, and the one that wins shows the ad. The networks built into the game are:

  • AppLovin · privacy policy
  • Unity Ads · privacy policy
  • Liftoff Monetize (Vungle) · privacy policy
  • Mintegral · privacy policy

The ads are:

  • Videos you choose to watch in exchange for a bonus: double a haul, one more drill, a rematch, scuffle tickets, a second helping of the daily crate, hatching an egg sooner or hurrying a prowl home. You can always decline, and declining costs nothing.
  • Ads between activities, from your second day and your third scuffle, after a drill result, a won scuffle or a won Weekly Rumble fight, at most once every three such moments, two minutes apart and six a day. The Starter Pack, No Ads and the Tamer's Edition turn these off.

There are no banner ads, and no ad is shown in your first session, on opening the game, during care, after a lost scuffle or Rumble fight, or before a haul has been shown.

To choose, show and measure ads, and to prevent fraud, AdMob and these networks collect information from your device, which may include:

  • the device model, iOS version, language, time zone and network type;
  • your IP address and the approximate location derived from it;
  • Apple's identifier for vendors (IDFV), and whether and how you interacted with an ad;
  • diagnostic and performance information about how the ad software is working;
  • Apple's advertising identifier (IDFA), only if you are 13 or over and tap "Allow" when iOS asks whether Grumbit may track you.

In the UK, Switzerland and the European Economic Area, Google's consent form is shown first, and you can reopen it from Settings → Privacy → Privacy choices (ads). Then, for players 13 and over, iOS shows Apple's tracking prompt, once, when you first open the game, straight after the age question and before you choose an egg. No ad is requested from any network until it is answered. If you choose Ask App Not to Track, ads still appear but are not personalised using the advertising identifier. You can change your answer at any time in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.

Google handles this data under its own policies: How Google uses information from apps that use its services · Google privacy policy. Each of the other networks handles the data under its own policy, linked above. The ad software contacts these networks' servers in the background to have an ad ready, whether or not one is then shown.

5. Children's privacy

Grumbit is for a general audience that includes children, and I treat players under 13 with extra care. It is not in the App Store's Kids Category, and I do not knowingly collect a child's name, email address, address, phone number, contacts, photos, precise location or any other personal information from a child. The game has no accounts, no chat, no way to share text or pictures with other players, and no social features of its own. For a player under 13 the game sends no Weekly Rumble score to Game Center and shows no leaderboard; achievements are still reported to Apple when the device is signed in to Game Center (section 6).

What the age question is for

It lets the game apply the rules below to players under 13 and to nobody else. It is a neutral question (no answer is suggested, and the year wheel starts on no year at all), and only the resulting age group is kept, on the device (section 1).

What changes for players under 13

  • Child-directed, non-personalised ads. Every ad request for this group is marked to Google as child-directed and as coming from a user under the age of consent. Ads are limited to content rated for general audiences, are not personalised, and the advertising identifier is not used.
  • No tracking prompt. The game never asks an under-13 player to allow tracking, so the advertising identifier is never made available to the ad networks. In the UK, Switzerland and the European Economic Area, where Google requires its own consent form, the form can still appear; for this group the request to Google is tagged as coming from under the age of consent.
  • No unprompted purchase offers. The three one-time offer cards (Starter Pack, No Ads, Tamer's Edition) are never put in front of an under-13 player. The Shop is there for whoever opens it, and every purchase on a child's device goes through Apple, where parents can require Ask to Buy (see section 7).
  • No notification prompt from the game. The game's own "may I send reminders?" card is never shown to an under-13 player. A grown-up can still turn reminders on in the game's Settings, and iOS's own controls always apply.
  • Analytics, limited. Gameplay statistics and crash reports still run (they tell me whether the game works), but for this group Firebase's ad-personalisation signals are turned off and its advertising storage is denied, so the statistics cannot be used to personalise ads. The random installation ID in section 3 is used only to run the game (internal operations), not to build a profile of a child. Parents or players can switch the statistics off under Settings → Privacy → Share gameplay statistics.

What the advertising and analytics partners receive

For an under-13 player, AdMob and the networks that bid through it may still receive the limited technical information needed to deliver, cap and measure an ad and to prevent fraud (the kinds of device and IP-address information in section 4), but not the advertising identifier, and not for personalised advertising. Google's rules for child-directed ad requests apply, and Google decides which ad networks may answer them. Firebase receives the gameplay events, the setting that turns ad personalisation off and the random installation ID described in section 3. If the device is signed in to Game Center, the game reports achievements to Apple exactly as it does for any player, but sends no Weekly Rumble score and shows no leaderboard (section 6); Apple's own rules for a child's Apple Account and Game Center apply. Nothing is sent that names, contacts or locates a child.

For parents

Use Apple's Ask to Buy and Screen Time to control purchases and what apps may do on a child's device. To stop a child's achievements being reported to Game Center, sign the device out of Game Center (iOS Settings → Game Center). You can also ask me what, if anything, is held about your child's use of the game; see section 10. If you believe a child under 13 has given me personal information, write to the address above and I will delete it.

6. Game Center

If you are signed in to Game Center, the game reports your achievements to Apple's Game Center under your Game Center profile, so they appear in Game Center. Once the Weekly Rumble is open in your game, and if you are 13 or over, it also posts your score for the week (a number worked out from the Rumble rounds you won) to the "Weekly Rumble" leaderboard, which starts afresh every Monday (UTC). Other Game Center players can see that score on the leaderboard under your Game Center name, as Apple shows it; the game sends only the number, never a monster's name. Nothing is reported while you are not signed in (if you sign in later, the game then reports the achievements you have and, if you are 13 or over, this week's score), and the game never asks you to sign in. For a player under 13 the game posts no Weekly Rumble score and shows no leaderboard; achievements are reported the same for every age group. To avoid posting the same score twice, the game keeps a note on your device of the last score it posted and for which Game Center player; that note does not leave the device. Grumbit has no Game Center multiplayer. Apple runs Game Center; sign out in iOS Settings → Game Center to stop it. Achievements and Rumble scores also exist inside the game whether or not you are signed in.

7. Purchases and Ask to Buy

No Ads, the Starter Pack, the Tamer's Edition and packs of gems are bought through Apple's App Store. Apple handles the payment; I never see your card, Apple Account or billing address. The game receives only a confirmation that a purchase completed, which it uses to deliver the item and to stop the same purchase being delivered twice. If you use Family Sharing and Ask to Buy, a parent approves each purchase on their own device. A record that a purchase was made (the product, and where in the game it was bought) is also one of the gameplay statistics in section 3, and Firebase's own automatic purchase event can add its price and currency.

8. iCloud

With iCloud on, your save (your monsters, coins, gems, purchases and collection) is kept in your own iCloud account so it follows you between devices. Apple stores it under your Apple Account; I cannot read it and it is not sent to any advertising or analytics partner. To stop it, turn off iCloud for Grumbit in iOS Settings; to delete it, use Settings → your name → iCloud → Manage Account Storage → Grumbit → Delete Data.

9. Reminders

Reminders are local notifications created on your device (a haul is back, an evolution happened, scuffle tickets are full), for each of your monsters, at most two a day in all. They need no account and no server; nothing about them is sent to me. The game's own card asks at most twice and iOS asks for permission only once; you can change it in the game's Settings or in iOS.

10. Your choices and deleting your data

  • Statistics and crash reports: switch off Settings → Privacy → Share gameplay statistics. Nothing more is sent from then on.
  • Tracking and personalised ads: iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking, and Settings → Privacy → Privacy choices (ads) where it is shown.
  • On your device: delete the game. That removes the save, the age group and the settings on the device. (The game's "Start over" button exists only in test builds and is not part of the App Store version.)
  • In iCloud: Settings → your name → iCloud → Manage Account Storage → Grumbit → Delete Data.
  • Game Center: sign out in iOS Settings → Game Center and nothing more is reported. Achievements and scores already reported are held by Apple under your Game Center account.
  • Data already sent to Google (Firebase): it is tied only to a random installation ID that the game does not show you, so I cannot look up one person's records by name or email. Switching statistics off and deleting the game ends collection, and old records are deleted after at most 14 months. You can write to the address above if you want me to try to remove records; I will say honestly what I can and cannot find.
  • Data held by the ad networks: use the controls in their policies linked in section 4.

12. Your rights

Depending on where you live, including under New Zealand's Privacy Act 2020, Australia's Privacy Act 1988, Canada's PIPEDA, the UK GDPR, the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection, Japan's Act on the Protection of Personal Information, the United States' Children's Online Privacy Protection Act and state laws such as California's CCPA, you may have the right to ask what data is held about you or your child, to have it corrected or deleted, and to object to its use. Because the game has no accounts, I hold nothing that identifies you by name; the statistics are tied only to a random installation identifier. To make a request, write to the address above (section 10 says what I can find). For data held by Google or the ad networks, use the controls in their policies.

Grumbit does not sell your personal information in exchange for money. An ad network's use of device data to show you ads can count as "sharing" for cross-context advertising under California law; you can limit it by choosing Ask App Not to Track, and it does not happen for players under 13.

13. Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new effective date. Significant changes will also be mentioned in the game's update notes.
