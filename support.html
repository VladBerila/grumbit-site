Raising your monster

How do I look after it?

Drag kibble from the bowl to the monster to feed it, tap a dropping to scoop it, and stroke the monster to pat it (hold still and it purrs). It sleeps on the schedule you set in Settings. A content monster grows stronger and tends to evolve into something good. Nothing here can kill it: on the usual rules it never dies and never runs away.

What is a care slip?

If one of its needs sits at the bottom for two waking hours, that counts as a slip, at most one a day. Slips are never shown as failure; they steer it towards the wild forms, which hit harder and guard less. A different build, not a worse one.

How does it evolve?

It grows with real time, from hatchling to rookie, prime and apex, and the forms it becomes depend on how you raised it: its mood, the stat you trained most, its care slips and its scuffle wins. A monster with a legacy can go one step further and become a Mythic. There are 40 forms in four lines, Ember, Moss, Tide and Storm. The Monsterpedia shows silhouettes and hints for the ones you have not found.

How do I get the Storm egg?

Beat the Stormridge boss on the Circuit and the Storm egg is yours, or unlock it early for 600 gems. After that, Storm is one of the eggs whenever you choose one: for an heir, or for a second monster.

Raising an heir, and the 1997 rules

Once your monster has beaten the whole Circuit, tap Raise an heir on the Circuit's champion panel or in the Den's More menu. From Apex on, Settings has a Retire card too. Your champion moves to the Hall of Legends and its heir's egg inherits a share of its strength. You can choose the 1997 rules for the heir: a monster neglected for too long can storm off. They apply only to that egg.

Two monsters

How do I get a second monster?

Three ways: beat the Rustyard boss (the Circuit's second), unlock it for 800 gems, or get the Tamer's Edition. It can move in once your first monster is a Rookie. Choose its egg and it hatches right there in the Den.

How do I switch between them?

Tap the other monster in the Den, or the little chip on the name plate (it is in the Train and Circuit headers too). Each has its own needs, Energy, tickets and Circuit lap; they share the Den, your coins and gems, the daily tasks and the Weekly Rumble. One can prowl while the other stays home to play.

Drills, scuffles and the Circuit

Drills

Smash trains Power, Brace trains Guard and Dash trains Speed. From the Rookie stage there is a fourth, Balance, a wobble board that trains Grit (a little extra strength in every stat); the day's first Balance run costs no Energy. Each drill takes about half a minute and costs Energy, so a rested monster manages a handful before it needs a nap. Drills get harder, and pay more, as your monster grows.

My stat is at its cap. Is there any point drilling it?

Yes. A drill on a capped stat is practice: no stat gain, but it still pays coins and Grit (full Grit for the first six practice drills of a day).

Snacks

The Train tab has a row of snacks, one a day. A snack makes the next drill of its stat gain one more. They cost coins.

Scuffles

A scuffle plays out on its own. You have one job: when your monster winds up its big move, tap Cheer as the ring closes for a critical hit. The Circuit has 45 rivals in five zones, with a boss and a trophy at the end of each. Each scuffle costs a ticket (you hold up to five; one comes back every 90 minutes). A loss to your next rival still pays a few coins and some Grit, and it never costs mood. With VoiceOver on, the Cheer window is wider and counts down aloud.

The Weekly Rumble

Beat the Circuit's first boss and a Rumble card appears on the Circuit. Each week brings a new bracket: a quarter-final, a semi-final and a final, a ticket a fight. Win the final for the week's prize to wear (or gems, if you have it already), and a tougher heat begins. Rounds you win add to your score for the week.

Where is the Rumble leaderboard?

On the Rumble screen, when you are signed in to Game Center. It is not shown to players under 13, and no score of theirs is sent. Your week's score goes to a Game Center leaderboard that starts afresh every Monday, and other players see it under your Game Center name. Not signed in? Nothing is sent, and the Rumble works just the same.

Prowls and the trail

Open the door to the Wilds to send your monster out for 1, 4 or 8 hours in a zone it has opened. It comes home with a haul of coins, Grit and sometimes a relic (there are 60 to find). If you have been away a few hours and nothing is waiting, it goes prowling on its own, so there is always something to come back to.

Following the trail

While it is out, the Wilds shows it walking the trail. About every two hours it stops for a moment: a fork in the path (pick coins, Grit or a chance at a relic), a cheer (tap in time, like in a scuffle) or a dig (junk for the workbench). Answer for extra loot. A moment stays open until the next one comes up, and missing one costs nothing.

Hurry home, or call it home

Hurry home brings it back now with the haul for the time it was out, the relic it was bringing and every moment you answered. It costs a video once a day, or 15 gems (twice a day), and needs at least half an hour left. Call it home is free and brings what it has found so far; a relic comes too only once it is halfway.

Do I lose scuffle tickets while it is out?

No. While it is on a prowl you sent, tickets keep coming back, up to three past the usual five. The Circuit shows them as "5 + 2" and so on.

Prowl gear

When you send it out you can pack one piece of gear, bought with coins: a compass (better odds of a relic), a lantern (half as much Grit again), a pack (twice the junk) or a map (a relic you do not have yet). The haul card says what it did.

Your Den

Junk and the workbench

Digs on the trail and prowls bring back junk. The Workbench (in the Shop and the Den's More menu) turns junk and coins into six Den pieces you cannot buy anywhere else.

Daily tasks

Tap the tasks chip in the Den to see today's three. Each pays coins as soon as it is done, and finishing all three adds 2 gems. New ones come each day.

Widgets

Touch and hold the Home Screen, tap + and search for Grumbit to add the small or medium widget; Lock Screen widgets are in the Lock Screen's customise menu. The buttons on the widget feed and pat your monster without opening the game. With two monsters, the widget shows the one you last picked in the game on that device. The widget shows what the game last saved and updates on a schedule iOS controls, so it can lag a little.

Reminders

After a haul (never in your first session), the game asks whether it may send reminders; if you say Not now, it may ask once more later. At most two a day in all: a haul is back, it has stopped on the trail, an evolution happened, or scuffle tickets are full, for each of your monsters; never at night or just after you played. You can turn each kind on or off in the game's Settings, or in the iPhone's Settings → Notifications → Grumbit. Players who said they are under 13 are not asked by the game; a grown-up can turn reminders on in Settings.

Your game on other devices

With iCloud on, your game follows you between your iPhone and iPad, both monsters included. Settings → iCloud sync says where it stands. If two devices played offline, the game merges them: coins and purchases are never counted twice. If one device runs an older version of Grumbit than the one that saved the game, it pauses syncing until you update.

Languages

Grumbit speaks nine languages: English, French, German, Italian, Spanish, Brazilian Portuguese, Japanese, Korean and Simplified Chinese. It follows your device's language; to change it for Grumbit alone, use the iPhone's Settings → Grumbit → Language.

Purchases

Grumbit sells seven things: No Ads, the Starter Pack, the Tamer's Edition (each bought once), and four packs of gems. Nothing sold is random, nothing expires, and nothing your monster needs is ever for sale. Gems buy Den themes, things to wear, the Storm egg early (600), a second monster early (800) and conveniences like scuffle tickets, hatching an egg now or hurrying a prowl home; the Den pieces on sale cost coins. The Tamer's Edition includes No Ads, every video bonus without watching, a second monster, the Lighthouse Den and 1,000 gems. Find everything in the Shop (the shop button at the top of the Den).

I bought something and didn't get it.

Close the game completely and open it again; the App Store sometimes confirms a purchase a little later, and the game picks it up at launch. If it is still missing, write to me with the date of the purchase.

I have a new phone. Can I get my purchases back?

No Ads, the Starter Pack and the Tamer's Edition come back with Settings → Restore purchases (also at the bottom of the Shop), and can be shared through Family Sharing. Gems are used up when spent and cannot be restored once spent. With iCloud on, your game itself, gems included, comes back from iCloud.

Purchases on a child's device

All payments go through Apple. Use Apple's Ask to Buy (Settings → Family on the parent's device) so a parent approves each purchase. Players who said they are under 13 are never shown a purchase offer by the game unasked, but the Shop stays open to whoever opens it.

Refunds

All payments go through Apple, so refunds do too: visit reportaproblem.apple.com.

Ads

Watching a video is always your choice: it can double a haul, hurry a prowl home, hatch an egg now, or give one more drill, a rematch, scuffle tickets or a second helping of the daily crate. If no video can be shown, you get a smaller bonus free (a rematch is the one exception). There are never ads in your first session, on opening the game, during care, after a lost fight or before a haul has been shown. From your second day, a few ads can appear between activities (after a drill result, a won scuffle or a won Rumble fight, at most six a day). No Ads, the Starter Pack and the Tamer's Edition turn those off. The optional videos stay, because you choose them (the Tamer's Edition gives their rewards without watching).

If a video doesn't load, check your connection and try again in a moment.

Your age group

The game asks for your year of birth once, before anything else, and keeps only an age group (under 13, 13 to 17, or adult) on your device. It changes how ads are requested and whether the game asks to track you; the privacy policy explains exactly what changes. If you are 13 or over you can correct your age group in Settings. The under-13 group is final on a device: if it was chosen by mistake, delete Grumbit and install it again (your game comes back from iCloud).

Privacy and deleting your data

To stop the game sending gameplay statistics and crash reports, switch off Settings → Privacy → Share gameplay statistics. To stop your achievements and Rumble score going to Game Center, sign out in the iPhone's Settings → Game Center. To remove the game from a device, delete the app; to remove the copy in iCloud, use iPhone Settings → your name → iCloud → Manage Account Storage → Grumbit → Delete Data. The details, and how ad data is handled, are in the privacy policy.

Accessibility

With VoiceOver on, the Den, the monster and the scuffle describe themselves and offer actions (feed, pat, scoop, Cheer). Settings has switches for Sound and Haptics, and the scuffle has a Reduce Motion version that follows the iPhone's setting. Tell me what gets in your way; I want to fix it.
